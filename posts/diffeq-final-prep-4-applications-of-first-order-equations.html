4. Applications of First-Order Equations

Oct 26, 2026 · DIFFEQ-FINAL-PREP · Differential Equations · Author: Archiveskk · Source: https://t.me/c/2310214877/1867/4793

Description

First-order equations model growth and decay, mixing, cooling, motion under resistance, and geometric families of curves. The skill here is setting up the equation from words.

4.1 Growth and Decay

Q′ = aQ gives Q(t) = Q₀e^(a(t − t₀)).

  • Decay uses a = −k with k > 0: Q = Q₀e⁻ᵏᵗ.
  • Half-life τ: k = (ln 2)/τ, equivalently Q = Q₀ 2^(−t/τ).
  • Interest compounded n times per year: Q(t) = Q₀(1 + r/n)ⁿᵗ. As n → ∞ this becomes continuous compounding

``text Q(t) = Q₀eʳᵗ. ``

  • Doubling time at continuous rate r: t = (ln 2)/r.
  • With a source or a drain, the model becomes linear: Q′ = −kQ + s (constant input s), with limit s/k.

4.2 Cooling and Mixing

Newton's law of cooling: T′ = −k(T − Tₘ), k > 0, so

T(t) = Tₘ + (T₀ − Tₘ)e⁻ᵏᵗ.

If the time of the initial state is unknown, use two measurements and take the ratio of the two equations to eliminate the unknown shift.

Mixing (tank) problems: for the amount Q(t) of solute,

Q′ = (rate in)(concentration in) − (rate out)Q/(V(t)),     V(t) = V₀ + (rᵢₙ − rₒᵤₜ)t.

This is a linear equation.

4.3 Elementary Mechanics

Newton's second law m v′ = F.

  • Falling body with linear drag: m v′ = mg − c v, so v(t) = (mg/c)(1 − e^(−ct/m)) when v(0)=0, and the terminal velocity is mg/c.
  • Position follows from y′ = v.
  • Use consistent units (for g = 9.8 m/s² or 32 ft/s²).

4.4 Autonomous Second-Order Equations

If y″ = F(y,y′) has no explicit x, set v = y′ so that y″ = vdv/(dy). The equation becomes first order in v as a function of y. Solve for v(y), then solve y′ = v(y).

  • For y″ = F(y): ½v² = ∫F(y) dy + C (an energy integral).

4.5 Applications to Curves

  • Orthogonal trajectories: if a family satisfies y′ = f(x,y), curves that cross it at right angles satisfy y′ = −1/(f(x,y)). To find the family's equation, differentiate and eliminate the parameter.
  • Geometric conditions (tangent length, normal length, area under the curve) are translated into an equation for y′.

Common Pitfalls

  • Mixing up the sign of k in decay and cooling formulas.
  • Forgetting that V(t) changes when the inflow and outflow rates differ.
  • Mixing units (minutes vs hours, grams vs kilograms).
  • Forgetting to eliminate the parameter before taking the negative reciprocal in orthogonal trajectories.

Practice Problems

  1. A substance has half-life 1250 years and Q(0)=40 g. Find the amount left after 500 years and the time needed to reach 5 g.
  2. A substance loses 10% of its mass in 6 days. Find its half-life.
  3. A bank pays 4.5% interest compounded continuously. (a) How long does it take for a deposit to triple? (b) What initial deposit grows to $50,000 in 12 years at 3.5%?
  4. A tank holds 400 L of brine containing 10 kg of salt. Brine with 0.2 kg/L enters at 5 L/min and the well-mixed solution leaves at 5 L/min. Find Q(t) and lim_(t → ∞) Q(t).
  5. Coffee at 90°C is placed in a 22°C room and cools to 70°C in 5 minutes. When will it reach 40°C?
  6. An object is placed outside where the temperature is −5°C. At 2:10 and 2:14 its temperature is 30°C and 18°C. At what time was it initially at 60°C?
  7. A 5 kg object falls from rest with air resistance 2v newtons. Find v(t) and the terminal velocity. Use g=9.8 m/s².
  8. A tank starts with 200 L of pure water. A solution with 0.1 kg/L enters at 4 L/min and the mixture leaves at 2 L/min. Write and solve the initial value problem for Q(t). State for how long the model is valid if the tank holds 500 L.
  9. Solve y″ = 4y³ with y(0) = 1, y′(0) = −√2 using v = y′.
  10. Find the orthogonal trajectories of the family y = Cx².

Conclusion

Most applications reduce to Q′ = (in) − (out) or "rate proportional to something". Write down the quantity, the rates and units first; the solution method is then one of the first-order techniques from Chapter 2.
