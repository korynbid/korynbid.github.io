9. Linear Higher-Order Equations

Oct 26, 2026 · DIFFEQ-FINAL-PREP · Differential Equations · Author: Archiveskk · Source: https://t.me/c/2310214877/1867/4793

Description

Everything from Chapter 5 extends to equations of order n:

P₀(x)y⁽ⁿ⁾ + P₁(x)y⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾ + … + Pₙ(x)y = F(x).

9.1 Theory

  • A homogeneous equation of order n has n linearly independent solutions y₁,…,yₙ, and its general solution is ∑cᵢyᵢ.
  • Wronskian: the determinant of the matrix with rows yᵢ, yᵢ′, …, yᵢ⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾. If it is nonzero at a point of the interval, the solutions are independent.
  • The IVP with n initial conditions y(x₀), y′(x₀),…,y⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾(x₀) has a unique solution when the coefficients are continuous and P₀ ≠ 0.
  • Nonhomogeneous: y = yₕ + yₚ.

9.2 Constant-Coefficient Homogeneous Equations

Use y = eʳˣ to get the characteristic polynomial of degree n.

  • Each simple real root r gives eʳˣ.
  • A real root of multiplicity m gives eʳˣ, xeʳˣ, …, xᵐ⁻¹eʳˣ.
  • A complex pair α ± iβ of multiplicity m gives xʲe^(αx)cos βx and xʲe^(αx)sin βx for j = 0,…,m−1.
  • To find roots of a cubic or quartic: try rational roots (factors of the constant term over factors of the leading coefficient), divide, then solve the remaining quadratic.

9.3 Undetermined Coefficients

Same trial functions as in Chapter 5. The resonance rule uses the multiplicity of the matching characteristic root: multiply the trial function by xˢ where s is that multiplicity (this can now exceed 2).

9.4 Variation of Parameters

Write yₚ = ∑uₖyₖ and impose

∑ₖ uₖ′yₖ⁽ʲ⁾ = 0  (j = 0,…,n−2),     ∑ₖuₖ′yₖ⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾ = F/P₀.

This is an n × n linear system for u₁′,…,uₙ′ (use Cramer's rule with Wronskian determinants), then integrate each uₖ′.

Common Pitfalls

  • Missing a repeated root (check that the number of solutions equals the order).
  • Mistakes in polynomial factoring; always verify a found root.
  • Forgetting to apply all n initial conditions.
  • In variation of parameters, using F instead of F/P₀.

Practice Problems

  1. y‴ − 6y″ + 11y′ − 6y = 0.
  2. y⁽⁴⁾ − 16y = 0.
  3. y⁽⁴⁾ + 8y″ + 16y = 0.
  4. y‴ − 2y″ − 5y′ + 6y = 0.
  5. y‴ − 3y″ + 2y′ = 0, y(0) = 1, y′(0) = 0, y″(0) = 2.
  6. y‴ − y″ − 4y′ + 4y = 6e²ˣ.
  7. y‴ + y′ = 4x − 2 sin x.
  8. y⁽⁴⁾ − y = 3eˣ.
  9. Verify that x, x², x³ solve x³y‴ − 3x²y″ + 6xy′ − 6y = 0 on x>0, show they are independent, and find a particular solution of x³y‴ − 3x²y″ + 6xy′ − 6y = x⁴ by variation of parameters.
  10. Compute the Wronskian of eˣ, xeˣ, e⁻²ˣ and state whether they are independent.

Conclusion

Higher order brings no new ideas, only more bookkeeping: an nth degree characteristic polynomial, n independent solutions, n initial conditions, and an n × n system in variation of parameters.
